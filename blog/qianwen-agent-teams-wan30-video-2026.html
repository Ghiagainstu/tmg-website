Qianwen Studio's Agent Teams + Wan 3.0: AI Short-Drama Production Goes End-to-End

Alibaba's Qianwen Studio opened Agent Teams to all users on August 31: screenwriter, director, art, camera and editor agents turn a story idea into a finished video on top of Wan 3.0 — the model that turns documents into 30-second native video. It arrives the day before China's micro-drama compliance rules take effect.

TMG
Tuyue Media Gateway
Paid Media Team · Shanghai

On August 31, 2026, Alibaba's AI content platform Qianwen Studio (千问创作) opened its Agent Teams feature to all users on PC and app — a multi-agent pipeline that takes a story idea and turns it into a finished video with screenwriter, director, art, cinematography and editing agents working in sequence. Under the hood it ships with Wan 3.0, the video generation model that can turn a PPT, PDF or even an Excel file directly into a 30-second native 1080p video. Combined with the micro-drama compliance rules that took effect September 1, this is the moment AI short-drama production in China goes from "prompt and pray" to a structured industrial pipeline — with labels built in.

1. What Happened: Agent Teams Goes Live

On August 31, Qianwen Studio — Alibaba's AI platform for professional content producers — announced that Agent Teams was open to all users on PC and mobile. The feature brings multi-agent collaboration to professional video creation: after a user describes a creative idea, specialized expert agents — screenwriter, director, art, cinematography, editor — take over in sequence to deliver a finished piece.

Key facts:

ItemDetail
LaunchAugust 31, 2026, all users (PC + App)
Base modelsWan 3.0 video (incl. Prime version) + Qwen-Image-3.0 image models
MechanismIdea → screenplay → storyboard → character design → video generation → editing
ScenariosAI short drama, comic-drama (漫剧), marketing ads, music videos

The positioning is explicit: lower the barrier to professional video creation by integrating the whole production chain — script, storyboard, generation, editing — into one AI workflow.

2. Agent Teams: The Pipeline

The core innovation is orchestration, not a single model. A user supplies the idea; a roster of role-specific agents executes each professional step:

RoleResponsibility
Screenwriter (编剧)Writes the script from the user's idea
Director (导演)Converts script into storyboard and shot planning
Art (美术)Sets character and scene design
Cinematography (摄影)Generates the video shots
Editor (剪辑)Assembles and finishes the cut

For marketing ads and music videos the same mechanism applies: idea → asset generation → assembly. The practical effect for content teams: what used to require a production crew and days of iteration can now be orchestrated by one operator with one concept.

3. Wan 3.0: Document-to-Video and the 30-Second Native Shot

Wan 3.0 is the production engine underneath. It moved from public beta (August 6) to full release on August 24, with two headline capabilities:

  • Native 30-second single generation: one continuous shot that can carry a complete narrative with complex camera moves — a category first for Chinese video models.
  • Document-to-video: the industry's first support for directly inputting doc, xls, ppt, pdf and md files as video sources. A marketing plan, a product spec, or a research deck becomes a video.
  • Multi-reference consistency: up to 20 reference materials (images, text, audio, video) can be combined, keeping characters, props and environments consistent across shots.

4. Where It Lands: Short Drama, Ads, MV, Travel

Reported production use cases:

  • Short drama / film: close-up facial shots that survive industrial-scale output; stable character consistency across episodes.
  • Advertising: retains product appearance and logo details — finished shots approaching real-photography quality.
  • Travel & tourism: city landmark promotional videos generated quickly.
  • Music: image input auto-generates multi-shot MVs with lip-synced lyrics.

The short-drama case is the most consequential for marketers: the AI short-drama format that has flooded Chinese platforms now has a production-grade toolchain behind it.

5. The Compliance Twist: 9/1 Rules Meet AI Production

This launch lands the day before China's first sectoral micro-drama regulation took effect (September 1, 2026). Three requirements directly intersect with AI-generated short-drama production:

  1. Per-episode AI labels: AI-generated episodes must carry a visible AI prompt in every episode — now a legal requirement, not an option.
  2. Tiered filing: micro-dramas are classified by investment/theme with filing and licensing requirements.
  3. Platform verification: platforms must flag suspected unlabeled AI content.

The practical implication: the production pipeline that Agent Teams industrializes must now also industrialize compliance — labels, provenance metadata and filing records become part of the workflow, ideally generated at the point of creation rather than retrofitted before publishing.

6. Why This Matters for Brands and Agencies

  1. Content cost structure changes again. A 30-second branded video at roughly ¥36 (list price, pre-discount) — or a full AI short-drama episode pipeline orchestrated by one operator — resets what "video content" costs for campaigns.
  2. Speed becomes a strategy. Document-to-video means a product launch deck can become a promotional video the same day. For agencies, that collapses lead times on localized Chinese content.
  3. Compliance is now a production input, not a review step. Teams building AI short-drama content must treat labels and filing as part of the pipeline — the brands that build this in first will outlast those retrofitting it under enforcement pressure.

7. What to Try Next

  • Test document-to-video with a real asset (a campaign brief, a product spec, a market report) and measure the quality gap to your current video production.
  • Run a short Agent Teams pilot for a micro-drama or ad concept, and document the workflow + per-episode AI label compliance in parallel.
  • Budget for 1080p only where needed: 480P/720P tiers exist for iteration loops; reserve 1080P for final delivery.
  • Watch the enforcement calendar: the first fines under the September 1 micro-drama rules will set precedents for AI-generated content labeling — track them before scaling production.

Takeaway: Qianwen Studio's Agent Teams plus Wan 3.0 turn AI video from a toy into an orchestrated production pipeline, just as China's first micro-drama regulation goes live. For brands and agencies producing Chinese content, the pair is a cost reset — and the compliance rules are the new gatekeeping factor that decides who scales.
