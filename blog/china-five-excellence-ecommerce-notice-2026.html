Why Double 11 Changed This Year: China's 'Five Excellence' E-Commerce Notice

Across the past several weeks the observable changes in China's e-commerce promotions have looked scattered: cross-store basket discounts disappearing in favour of a single official reduction, JD cancelling deposit pre-sale, and platforms redirecting investment into AI operating tools and fulfilment rather than deeper discounts. Those changes are not independent platform choices. They follow a single seven-department notice, signed on 11 September and released on 10 October, days before Double 11 opened. This article reads the three clauses that map onto the observed changes, sets out the notice's five dimensions and fifteen measures, and works through what it changes for brands planning Q4 and the next 618.

Those changes are not independent platform choices. They follow a single seven-department notice.
Document《关于实施品质电商"五优"行动的通知》 — Notice on Implementing the Quality E-Commerce "Five Excellence" Campaign
Issuing departmentsSeven: Ministry of Commerce; Cyberspace Administration of China; National Development and Reform Commission; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology; Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs; State Administration for Market Regulation; State Post Bureau
Document date11 September 2026
Public release10 October 2026, via the Ministry of Commerce E-Commerce Department
Legal basis《提振消费专项行动方案》, the special action plan for boosting consumption
Structure15 numbered measures across five dimensions

The stated principle is that consumption promotion and consumer welfare are to be pursued together, that regulating the market and improving quality carry equal weight, and that digital commerce and the real economy should reinforce each other — towards a cycle of quality supply, quality service and quality consumption.

2. The Clause That Explains This Year's Double 11

Three clauses in the notice map almost exactly onto the changes advertisers have been observing, and they are worth reading before the rest.

Measure (14), on disorderly competition, orders enforcement against e-commerce platforms' "automatic price-matching" and "lowest price on the entire web" mechanisms as disorderly competitive behaviour, and directs that platform commission structures and merchant rating rules be standardised to avoid squeezing the survival space of quality merchants.

Measure (2), on platform rules, requires platforms to optimise their product display, recommendation and marketing mechanisms, reinforcing quality signals such as product quality, service reputation and credit information. Two of the three things named — display and recommendation — sit directly in the ranking logic that decides what a consumer sees.

Measure (7), on consumption events, states that around consumption nodes such as 618 and Double 11, platforms should be guided and regulated by coordinating the efforts of all parties.

Taken together these explain the pattern. Discount-stacking mechanics that existed mainly to game a price comparison were the target; a simpler official reduction is the compliant alternative. Investment shifting to fulfilment and AI operating efficiency is the direction the notice rewards.

3. The Five Dimensions, and What Each One Does

The notice is not solely a price-war crackdown. It is a fifteen-measure programme with five distinct workstreams, and the price provisions are one of them.

DimensionMeasuresFocus
Platform quality leadership (品质推优)1–3Platform commitments, rule reform, end-to-end quality control, chief quality officer
Merchant quality upgrading (品质创优)4–6Merchant capability, own-brand development, industrial-belt access, trade-to-domestic-market conversion
Consumer-side quality selection (品质择优)7–9Consumption events, online-offline experience, logistics integration, consumer finance
Silk Road e-commerce quality (品质争优)10–12Cross-border governance, overseas brand programme, rules alignment
Standards and long-term protection (长效护优)13–15Standards system, disorderly-competition enforcement, industry self-regulation

Two things are notable about the structure. First, the competition provisions sit in a dimension titled "long-term protection" — enforcement is framed as protecting quality merchants rather than as a consumer-price measure. Second, merchant-side measures (4–6) are as substantial as the platform-side ones, which is unusual for a promotional-practice document.

4. Platform Rules Are a Named Target

Measure (2) is the clause most likely to be underweighted by advertisers, because it regulates the mechanism rather than the message.

It directs platforms to publish rules and to solicit public comment before making or amending them, and to standardise how rules are executed in order to protect merchants and consumers. It then requires platforms to continuously optimise product display, recommendation and marketing mechanisms, reinforcing quality-oriented signals: product quality, service reputation and credit information.

For a brand, that is a statement about ranking inputs. Marketing mechanisms are named alongside display and recommendation, which places promotional exposure inside the scope of the reform rather than outside it.

Measure (3) adds an infrastructure layer: platforms are guided to appoint a chief quality officer, and government data verification is to be extended so that platforms can verify merchant licence information against government records. Electronic certification services from approved providers are encouraged. The practical direction is toward credential verification against authoritative registries rather than platform-internal trust signals.

5. Why "Automatic Price-Matching" Was the Trigger

The notice names two mechanisms: automatic price-matching and lowest price on the entire web. The policy commentary around the release explains the harm model, and it is worth stating precisely because it is the rationale an advertiser will be asked about.

According to the municipal guidance published alongside the notice, these mechanisms produced merchant price cannibalisation, quality-cutting, and compressed service costs, squeezing the survival space of quality-compliant merchants. The failure is not that prices fell; it is that a mechanism forced prices toward a floor that quality-compliant merchants could not meet without degrading the product.

The Ministry of Commerce Research Institute researcher Hong Yong framed the intent as shifting e-commerce from scale expansion to quality improvement, noting that the measures aim to optimise platform recommendation and marketing mechanisms so that business models relying purely on low price and traffic competition are changed, giving merchants that invest in product innovation, quality and brand building more opportunity.

That sentence is the operative one for a marketer. The notice does not prohibit discounting. It devalues discounting as a sole competitive strategy.

6. What Platforms Have Already Changed

The Q4 platform announcements show the direction the notice rewards. These are platform decisions, not requirements of the notice, but they align with it closely enough to be read as a response.

PlatformMove
JDCancelled deposit pre-sale and opened with the spot sale on 12 October; upgraded the Jingmai AI operations centre with 60-plus AI tools spanning store operations, product selection, marketing and order fulfilment; send-and-install average waiting time compressed to 0.88 days
Taobao / TmallAlibaba Marketing's AI targeting product Wanxiang Dianjing; several billion RMB of platform-funded smart coupons; expanded Double 11 to 20 overseas markets with RMB 1.2 billion of overseas marketing resources
DouyinPlatform-funded consumption coupons distributed across seven ByteDance apps including Douyin, Doubao and Hongguo Short Drama, linking content discovery to checkout
Tmall and JDBoth scheduled their Double 11 press conferences for 15 October — the same day, breaking a long-standing practice of staggered announcements

The common thread is investment in operating efficiency and fulfilment rather than in headline discount depth. JD measured itself on install waiting time; Taobao measured itself on overseas market coverage; Douyin measured itself on cross-app distribution. None led with a discount rate.

7. The Standards and Self-Regulation Layer

Two further measures sit underneath enforcement and deserve separate attention, because they change what compliance will mean over time.

Measure (13) establishes an "e-commerce standards benefit enterprise" action, building out a quality e-commerce standards system, focusing standards development on how products and services are provided and how their quality is assured. It adds standards promotion, implementation supervision, and a requirement for enterprises to benchmark against the standards — described as a cycle of standards leading and quality following.

Measure (15) moves to organise a national e-commerce industry association, working with consumer associations, and to use complaint, dispute-resolution and consumer-satisfaction data to build industry self-regulation. Industry bodies are to develop codes of conduct and integrity covenants and to host government-enterprise dialogue and dispute mediation platforms.

The sequence matters. Enforcement against named practices is immediate; standards and self-regulation are the durable layer that will determine what "quality" is verifiable against. Brands that treat this as a 2026 promotional-cycle issue rather than a multi-year direction are likely to be behind.

11. Key Takeaways

  • Seven departments issued 《关于实施品质电商"五优"行动的通知》, signed 11 September 2026 and released 10 October 2026 — the Ministry of Commerce, Cyberspace Administration, NDRC, MIIT, Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs, SAMR and the State Post Bureau.
  • The notice rests on 《提振消费专项行动方案》 and sets out 15 measures across five dimensions: platform quality leadership, merchant quality upgrading, consumer-side quality selection, Silk Road e-commerce quality, and standards and long-term protection.
  • Measure (14) orders correction of platforms' "automatic price-matching" and "lowest price on the entire web" mechanisms as disorderly competition, and directs standardisation of commission structures and merchant rating rules to avoid squeezing quality merchants.
  • Measure (2) requires platforms to optimise product display, recommendation and marketing mechanisms, reinforcing product quality, service reputation and credit information as quality signals.
  • Measure (7) names 618 and Double 11 explicitly, directing that platforms be guided and regulated around those consumption nodes.
  • Policy commentary attributes the problem to merchant price cannibalisation, quality-cutting and compressed service costs squeezing quality-compliant merchants.
  • A Ministry of Commerce Research Institute researcher framed the intent as moving e-commerce from scale expansion to quality improvement, so that business models relying purely on low price and traffic competition are changed.
  • Measure (13) builds an e-commerce standards system focused on how products and services are provided and their quality assured, with enterprise benchmarking.
  • Measure (15) moves to organise a national e-commerce industry association and to use complaint, dispute and satisfaction data for industry self-regulation.
  • Measure (10) includes anti-monopoly compliance guidance for e-commerce going overseas — cross-border conduct is addressed in a domestic instrument.
  • The Q4 platform announcements align with the direction: JD cancelling deposit pre-sale and investing in 60-plus AI tools and 0.88-day send-and-install, Taobao adding AI targeting and RMB 1.2 billion of overseas marketing resources, Douyin distributing coupons across seven ByteDance apps.
  • The notice does not prohibit discounting. It devalues discounting as a sole competitive strategy, and shifts verifiable quality toward the centre of platform mechanics.
  • This article is a policy summary and regulatory analysis for information purposes and is not legal advice. The measures are implemented by the issuing departments and platforms, and specific obligations should be confirmed with counsel and with the platforms concerned.
