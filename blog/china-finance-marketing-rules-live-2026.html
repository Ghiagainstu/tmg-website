China's Finance Marketing Rules Are Live: What the First Week Changed for Advertisers

The Administrative Measures for Online Marketing of Financial Products took effect on 30 September 2026, and the effects were visible within days. What makes the sequence unusual is that the platforms did not wait. Xiaohongshu published its finance convention on 15 September, Douyin cancelled all 6,888 Wealth Advisor verifications on 16 September, and Weibo tightened finance creator verification on 18 September - all before the rules took effect. Three things changed at once. Marketing content was withdrawn, with loan intermediaries bulk-deleting posts from Moments and WeChat groups. Bundled finance offers were unbundled, with the three major telecoms suspending zero-cost handset instalment offers and payment pages separating credit from payment tools. And private channels were brought inside the perimeter. This article takes the regulation as given and looks at what the first week revealed.

1. What Changed on 30 September

The Administrative Measures for Online Marketing of Financial Products (《金融产品网络营销管理办法》) took effect on 30 September 2026. Eight departments issued it — the People's Bank of China, MIIT, SAMR, the National Financial Regulatory Administration, the CSRC, CNIPA, the Cyberspace Administration and SAFE — and the effect was visible within days rather than months.

Three things changed at once, and each of them is easy to miss if you only read the rulebook:

  1. The grey supply of financial marketing content was withdrawn. Loan intermediaries deleted their marketing from social platforms, the platforms cancelled entire creator verification categories, and influencer verification was narrowed to licence holders.
  2. Bundled finance products were unbundled at the point of sale. Payment pages were restructured to separate credit from payment tools, and the major telecoms suspended zero-cost handset instalment offers entirely.
  3. Private channels were brought inside the perimeter. Moments posts and WeChat groups are now covered, not just public content.

What makes this unusual is the sequence. The platforms did not wait for the rules. They acted up to two weeks before the effective date.

2. The Timeline That Matters: Platforms Moved 15 Days Early

DateAction

16 SepDouyin announced it was cancelling all 6,888 "Wealth Advisor" (理财师) verifications; accounts posing the highest risk or already causing user financial loss would be permanently banned

Read the top four rows and the direction of travel is clear. Platforms treated the regulation as a competitive move rather than a compliance chore, and started cleaning up while advertisers were still reading the consultation drafts.

3. The Visible Effects

These are the concrete, observable changes that any brand operating in China should know about.

Marketing content disappeared. In early September, loan intermediary feeds were still full of "approvals closing soon", "low interest", "no credit check" posters. By late September, a Securities Times reporter randomly checked several intermediaries' Moments and found the posters almost entirely gone — replaced by blank feeds or a few personal posts. A loan broker in Shenzhen, three years in the role, described the instruction: clear all loan marketing from Moments and WeChat groups, including previously posted screenshots, because they were worried about being screenshotted and reported. The phrase doing the rounds in the industry was: "we used to fish with Moments; now the rods have been confiscated."

Bundled offers were withdrawn. The three major telecoms suspended zero-cost handset purchase offers across the board, because bundling a financial product requires prominent notice and default-consent is prohibited.

Checkout pages were restructured. Previously some payment platforms showed loan products alongside balances and bank cards, using situational traffic to drive what was effectively impulse borrowing. Article 12 forbids non-bank payment institutions from listing loans or asset-management products as payment options or marketing them at all. In practice, Taobao's checkout now puts Huabei in a separate credit column, Yu'ebao in a wealth column, and debit and credit cards together in a bank-card column. WeChat Pay and Alipay have made comparable changes.

A certification category was deleted. Douyin's removal of the "Wealth Advisor" verification category eliminated a credential that bloggers had packaged as professional endorsement. Some had used it to run illegal stock recommendations and divert users to private channels. Once the badge was gone, the associated tactic lost its value.

5. Three Ways People Responded — and Only One of Them Works

The most revealing evidence came from how market participants behaved, not from the announcements.

ResponseWhat happenedWhere it stands now
Passive complianceLoan intermediaries deleted marketing on instruction, including old screenshotsCompliant, but the channel is gone
Active evasionSome creators changed the registered entity overnight, swapping an attached media company for a private fund company; others stopped free content and kept only paid communitiesA new variant that platforms are still catching
Grey-market workaroundsOne creator reportedly paid RMB 2,400 to a grey intermediary for affiliation with a "licensed institution" to obtain verification — an institution with no trace in the AMAC registryFragile, and the registry is public

The distinction that matters is simple. A yellow-verified badge was never a licence, a profit screenshot was never a qualification, and follower count was never professional endorsement. Regulation did not change that; it made it enforceable.

6. The Grey Market: What Certification Intermediaries Are Charging Now

The compliance tightening pushed demand into a shadow supply chain, and its price list is now on the record.

Grey serviceReported price
"Guest professor" title for use in finance contentRMB 50,000 to 160,000
Institution affiliation (挂靠) serviceFrom RMB 1,000
One-off "pseudo-licensed institution" affiliation to obtain platform verificationReported at RMB 2,400

How the second-tier of this market now operates is instructive. Several certification-service intermediaries told reporters that fund yellow-verification assistance had been fully suspended, with the explanation that platform review is now strict. Others said verification at one platform might still be "worth a try", but only with a name match — the intermediary asks for a name, date of birth and gender, and if they match an existing record, the application proceeds.

That last detail is the one to watch. It is not sophisticated evasion; it is opportunistic use of publicly available personal data against a verification check that was not designed to withstand it. The registered institution in one reported case does not exist in the AMAC registry at all.

7. What the Rules Require of Licensed Institutions

The burden on compliant institutions is real, and it is worth stating plainly rather than only describing what was banned.

ObligationPractical meaning
Head-office-run approval and reviewMarketing content must be reviewed and archived; a branch or outsourced call centre can no longer amend copy at will
Written platform agreementsScope, responsibilities, client protection, data security, dispute resolution and termination transition must be documented
No sub-commissioningWork cannot be passed to another intermediary, formally or in substance
No disguised educationCommissioning platforms for "investor education" or "training courses" as a marketing route is prohibited, including the associated payment
Contract-matched contentProduct name, provider, seller, category, rates and risk warnings must match the financial product contract
Named practitionersAnyone marketing via livestream, short video or official accounts must be an employee of the institution, appropriately qualified and authorised

One city commercial bank illustrates the operational shift. It split its internet marketing content into four gates — product wording, compliance review, channel distribution, and documented review — and removed branch and call-centre staff's ability to modify copy independently. Several brokerages began re-examining whether their existing platform arrangements were compliant.

8. The New Baseline: Full-Category Rules and Practitioner Licensing

Two structural features of the regime are easy to overlook and will shape media planning for longer than the first-week headlines.

Coverage is uniform across categories. The Measures apply across deposits, loans, wealth management, insurance, securities and payments. Xue Hongyan, a financial policy researcher at Renmin University of China, made the point that a single cross-category rule set gives consumers a consistent protection baseline, removing the protection gaps that arose when different product types were governed separately — and pushes the industry's competitive focus away from copywriting and back to product risk control and service quality.

Practitioner licensing is now visible. "Licensed operation" was extended to "licensed practitioners". Marketing personnel must be institution employees with the relevant qualification and institutional authorisation. The livestream page becoming a place where the practitioner registration number is displayed is a small change with a large symbolic effect: the credential is now in front of the consumer at the moment of decision.

9. What This Means for International Brands

Five implications:

  1. Overseas expansion is now an explicit enforcement category. Douyin named it as one of three concentrations of violations, alongside credential problems and non-compliant marketing. Marketing financial products to PRC residents from an offshore entity is being treated as a specific target, not a grey area.
  2. Private channels are inside the perimeter. Moments posts and WeChat groups are covered. Creator marketing plans built on WeChat-group distribution need a compliance review they previously did not require.
  3. Creator selection has changed from audience to credential. With yellow-verification categories removed or narrowed, the question is no longer how large a creator's audience is but whether the person is verifiably licensed and authorised.
  4. Payment and credit can no longer be presented together. For any brand running consumer credit or instalment offers, the checkout design itself is now a compliance surface, not a conversion surface.
  5. The verification check is the weak link. The name-match workaround shows that identity verification is being defeated with public data. Brands relying on a third party's verification should assume it will be re-checked.

11. Key Takeaways

  • The Administrative Measures for Online Marketing of Financial Products took effect on 30 September 2026, issued by eight departments, and the first-week effects were visible immediately.
  • The platforms moved up to 15 days early. Xiaohongshu published its convention on 15 September, Douyin cancelled all 6,888 "Wealth Advisor" verifications on 16 September, and Weibo tightened finance creator verification on 18 September — all before the rules took effect.
  • The visible effects: intermediary marketing content deleted, telecoms suspending zero-cost handset offers, and payment pages separating credit from payment tools.
  • Platform action at scale: over 1.5 million pieces of finance content and over 160,000 accounts actioned year to date across platforms; Douyin 6,888 verifications cancelled; Tencent 53 ads taken down; Xiaohongshu over 1.01 million posts rectified in six months.
  • Certification grey-market prices are now public: guest-professor titles at RMB 50,000–160,000, affiliation services from RMB 1,000, and reported RMB 2,400 paid for affiliation with a non-existent "licensed institution".
  • Overseas expansion is an explicit enforcement category, named by Douyin alongside credential problems and non-compliant marketing.
  • Private channels are inside the perimeter — Moments and WeChat groups are covered, not only public content.
  • Coverage is uniform across deposits, loans, wealth management, insurance, securities and payments, giving consumers a consistent baseline and pushing competition back toward product risk control and service quality.
  • The baseline has shifted from "licensed operation" to "licensed practitioners", with qualification and authorisation made visible at the point of decision.
