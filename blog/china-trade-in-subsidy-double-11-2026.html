China's Trade-In Subsidy Meets Double 11: Why the 15% Is Not 15%

The state trade-in subsidy is now the largest single variable in Chinese e-commerce pricing, and it landed in the same window as Double 11. The final RMB 62.5 billion tranche has been fully disbursed, completing RMB 250 billion across four batches and covering 29 provinces until 31 December 2026. The headline rate is 15% of the amount actually paid, capped at RMB 1,500 per item for six appliance categories and RMB 500 for four digital categories. Because the rate is applied and then capped, the effective rate is not flat: on digital devices it falls from 15% to 8.3% across the eligible price range and to zero above RMB 6,000. This article works through the arithmetic that most promotional coverage omits, and sets out what it changes for Q4 planning.

1. What the Subsidy Actually Is

The state trade-in subsidy is now the largest single variable in Chinese e-commerce pricing, and it landed in the same window as Double 11. The final tranche of 2026 funding — RMB 62.5 billion, the fourth of four — has been fully disbursed, completing a full-year total of RMB 250 billion. It covers 29 provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities, is funded from ultra-long special treasury bonds, deployed jointly by the National Development and Reform Commission, the Ministry of Finance and the Ministry of Commerce, and runs until 31 December 2026.

Two categories are in scope nationally, with different caps:

Home appliances (6 categories)Digital devices (4 categories)
CategoriesAir conditioner, refrigerator, washing machine, TV, water heater, computerPhone, tablet, smartwatch, smart glasses
Rate15% of the amount actually paid15% of the amount actually paid
Per-item capRMB 1,500RMB 500
Price ceilingNone beyond the capRMB 6,000 list price
QualificationEnergy or water efficiency grade 1Compliant model under RMB 6,000

The subsidy is applied as an automatic deduction at checkout. No advance outlay and no post-hoc reimbursement are required, and the manufacturer's warranty and national service coverage are unaffected.

2. The Cap Creates a Kinked Curve, Not a Flat Rate

The headline is "15%". The effective rate is not 15%, and the difference is large enough to change what a "discounted price" means.

Because the rate is applied to the amount actually paid and then capped, the effective subsidy rate peaks at the point where 15% equals the cap, and declines from there:

Device typePrice at which the cap bindsEffective rate at the capEffective rate at the price ceiling
Digital (RMB 500 cap)RMB 3,33315%8.3% at RMB 6,000
Home appliance (RMB 1,500 cap)RMB 10,00015%15% maintained to RMB 10,000; declines above

For a digital device this means the curve has a hard shape. Below roughly RMB 3,333 the buyer receives the full 15%. Between RMB 3,333 and RMB 6,000 the nominal subsidy is frozen at RMB 500 and the effective rate falls continuously — a RMB 5,999 phone receives RMB 500, which is 8.3%, not 15%. Above RMB 6,000 the category is excluded entirely.

The practical consequence is that "15% off" is not a uniform price advantage across a catalogue. Two phones at RMB 3,000 and RMB 5,999 receive the same absolute subsidy while differing in effective rate by more than six percentage points.

3. Why Computers Are the Most Misunderstood Category

One classification detail is worth isolating because multiple sources describe it as the single most frequently misstated rule of the year: computers sit in the home-appliance category, not the digital category.

Consequence
Cap appliedRMB 1,500, not RMB 500
RMB 6,000 price ceilingDoes not apply
Practical effectHigh-price laptops and desktops can take the full appliance subsidy

A laptop priced at RMB 10,000 receives up to RMB 1,500. A phone at the same price receives nothing. The category boundary, not the price, determines eligibility — and it runs counter to the intuition that "computer" belongs with "phone and tablet."

Qualification also requires energy-efficiency grade 1 and a completed product filing. Only the filed configuration tier enters scope, so a model offered in several specifications may have only one eligible variant.

4. The Stacking Order Is Counter-Intuitive

The subsidy stacks with platform promotions rather than replacing them — but the order of operations is the opposite of what most stacking advice assumes.

Because the subsidy is calculated as 15% of the amount actually paid, reducing the amount paid increases the subsidy's absolute value only up to the cap. The commonly recommended sequence is therefore: apply platform coupons and red packets first to compress the amount actually paid, then let the subsidy be calculated on the reduced figure.

Two constraints sit on top of that. One item per category per person per year, with category quotas independent of each other. And a returned or cancelled order, or a change to the delivery address, voids the subsidy qualification for that category for the remainder of the year — a materially harsher rule than most platform-side price-protection policies.

The cumulative figures explain why this is now a structural pricing input rather than a promotion:

The quota mechanics matter as much as the totals. Funding is accounted for by province, and a province closes its channel early once its quota is consumed. The last batch is by definition the scarcest, and it expires in under eleven weeks.

6. What Is Excluded, and Why That Matters

The exclusion list is where most consumer-side confusion originates, and it maps directly onto product categories a brand might assume are covered:

ExcludedNote
Appliances at energy/water efficiency grade 2 or belowGrade 1 required
Digital devices priced above RMB 6,000Includes flagship phones and high-spec laptops priced as digital
Refurbished and second-hand devicesBoth excluded outright
Non-domestic (overseas-spec) devicesExcluded
Accessories and peripheralsExcluded

For a brand, the operative question is not "does the subsidy apply to my category" but "does it apply to this SKU at this price with this efficiency grade and this filing status". A catalogue can be 80% covered and still have its highest-margin SKUs entirely outside the scheme.

7. Eligibility Mechanics: Address, Identity, One Per Category

The qualification flow is short but has three hard gates that fail silently:

  1. Delivery address must fall within one of the 29 subsidised provinces.
  2. Real-name identity, recipient and payer must be consistent. A mismatch prevents the subsidy from appearing at checkout.
  3. One item per category per year, with categories counted independently.

The subsidy is claimed through platform search entry points — on JD, a search term routes the user to the subsidy venue, where identity and address are verified, category qualification is granted, and the deduction is applied automatically at checkout. Qualification is claimed before purchase, not reimbursed after.

8. The Coupon-Code Problem for Advertisers

A detail with direct search-marketing consequences: the entry points are search coupon codes, and they are not stable.

Coverage across early-October material references at least four different JD entry terms for the same subsidy venue. The codes rotate, they are promotion-specific, and they are published in content that is itself template-generated at volume. One analysis of the current wave is explicit that entry-point reliability ranks as: official platform accounts and venue pages, then in-app coupon centres and membership pages, then third-party guide articles. Code pages frequently point at promotional placements rather than official entries.

For a paid media planner this creates a specific trap. Coupon-code search terms generate high-intent, high-conversion volume during the window — but the volume is attached to a rotating, non-owned entry point. A campaign built on bidding a specific code inherits the code's lifecycle, and the code will change when the promotion changes.

11. Key Takeaways

  • The final RMB 62.5 billion tranche has been fully disbursed, completing RMB 250 billion across four batches in 2026, covering 29 provinces and running to 31 December 2026.
  • The subsidy is 15% of the amount actually paid, capped — RMB 1,500 per item for six appliance categories, RMB 500 per item for four digital categories.
  • The effective rate is not flat. For digital devices the cap binds at RMB 3,333; a RMB 5,999 phone receives RMB 500, an effective 8.3%. Above RMB 6,000 the category is excluded.
  • Computers are classified as appliances, not digital: they take the RMB 1,500 cap and are exempt from the RMB 6,000 ceiling. Multiple sources describe this as the most frequently misstated rule of the year.
  • Appliances require energy or water efficiency grade 1 and a completed product filing; only the filed configuration tier is eligible.
  • Excluded: grade-2-or-below appliances, digital devices above RMB 6,000, refurbished and second-hand devices, overseas-spec devices, and accessories.
  • Stacking order matters. The subsidy is calculated on the paid amount, so compressing the paid amount first can increase the subsidy's absolute value up to the cap.
  • One item per category per person per year, and a return, cancellation or address change voids the annual category qualification.
  • Cumulative effect to August 2026: RMB 1.55 trillion in sales, 208 million people, 91.458 million appliance units.
  • Entry points are rotating search codes. Reliability ranks official venue pages above in-app coupon centres above third-party guides, and code pages often point at promotional placements.
  • This article is a policy and platform-mechanism summary for information purposes and is not legal, tax or compliance advice. Subsidy categories, caps, provincial quotas and platform mechanics are set by the issuing authorities and platforms and change without notice; verify current terms in the official venue before committing budget.
