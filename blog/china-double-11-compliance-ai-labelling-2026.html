China's Double 11 Compliance Wave: AI Advertising Labelling Moves From Rule to Enforcement

Double 11 opened on the evening of 30 October. Within seventy-two hours, three separate Chinese market regulators published compliance documents — not before the campaign, but while it was running. Sichuan-Chongqing split livestream liability into four duty holders, Guangzhou set out eight requirements including an explicit AI-labelling line, and Jinan addressed pop-up ads. The AI labelling obligation itself is not new: it took effect on 1 September 2025. What changed is when it is enforced, and a consumer alert published on 1 November documents exactly how brands have been evading it.

1. Three Documents in Three Days

Double 11 opened on the evening of 30 October. Within seventy-two hours, three separate market regulators published compliance documents — not before the campaign, but while it was running.

DateIssuerInstrumentScope
30 OctSichuan + Chongqing market regulators (joint)Double 11 centralised promotion compliance guidancePlatform duties, four livestream duty holders, product and IP controls
31 OctGuangzhou Municipal Market Supervision AdministrationAdministrative guidance opinion on Double 11 promotion conduct, eight areasPricing, algorithms, AI labelling, anti-competitive conduct, three-year records
30 OctJinan Municipal Market Supervision AdministrationCompliance guide on pop-up ads in car-park QR payment pagesAd identification, one-click close, four-party liability

Read individually, each is a routine local notice. Read together, they describe a shift in when Chinese advertising compliance happens.

The important detail is not any single clause. It is that the guidance arrived mid-festival. A brand that launched creative on 30 October was audited against documents published on 31 October.

2. The Rule That Was Already in Force

The AI labelling requirement that Guangzhou restates is not new. It comes from the Measures for Labelling AI-Generated Synthetic Content, issued jointly by the Cyberspace Administration of China and three other departments, effective 1 September 2025.

ElementRequirement
Covered contentText, images, audio, video and virtual scenes generated or synthesised by AI
Label typesExplicit labels (visible to the user) and implicit labels (embedded metadata)
Video specificallyA prominent notice must appear in the opening frame and in the area surrounding playback
Obligation holderWhoever generates or distributes the content — the platform is not the only party responsible

This distinction is what most coverage misses. The 2025 measures created the obligation. The 2026 Double 11 documents are about enforcement of an obligation that already existed, and the consumer alert published on 1 November shows exactly how brands have been sidestepping it.

So the story is not "China introduced AI labelling rules." It is "China started checking."

3. Guangzhou: Eight Requirements, One New Line

Guangzhou's administrative guidance opinion sets out eight areas. Most are restatements of existing law; the operating value is in seeing them enumerated together as a single audit checklist.

#AreaCore requirement
1Platform responsibilityRe-verify merchant credentials, remove unlicensed or discredited sellers; disclose promotion rules, deposits, price protection and coupon terms clearly; no "choose one of two"; no restricting merchant pricing
2Algorithm and trafficRegulate algorithmic distribution; prohibit big-data price discrimination; AI marketing and digital-human livestreams must prominently disclose that the content is AI-generated
3PricingClear price marking; no fabricated original prices, no raising prices before discounting, no false comparisons; state the discount baseline; publish how multiple discounts stack and in what order
4Subsidy transparencyDistinguish product price, platform discount and government subsidy as separate line items
5Advertising and livestreamTruthful claims; no inflated efficacy, no fake reviews or fabricated sales volumes; livestream promises must match the product page; AI deep-synthesis promotional content must be labelled as required; special categories (pharmaceuticals, devices, health food) need pre-clearance
6Product quality and IPNo quality reduction through promotion; verify IP authorisation; proactive online sampling
7Fulfilment and after-salesSeven-day no-reason returns and three-guarantee obligations; no unreasonable return thresholds; no forced bundling; pre-sale items shipped on schedule
8Records and internal controlPrice records, livestream replays, orders and complaint files retained for at least three years; internal compliance roles; pre-review of scripts

Two of these are new to most brand playbooks: the AI labelling line in area 2, and the three-year retention in area 8. The rest are familiar obligations that are now being listed as things to check during, not after, the campaign.

4. Chongqing–Sichuan: Four Livestream Duty Holders

The joint Sichuan-Chongqing guidance does something the national rules leave implicit: it splits livestream liability into four distinct duty holders.

Duty holderObligation
Livestream platformManage rooms, hosts and agencies; dynamic content monitoring; issue warnings, throttle traffic or suspend streams on violation; retain disposal records
Room operator (the brand or its agency)Display operator identity, prices and service items prominently and continuously; vet product selection and marketing language; manage live interaction in real time
Host agencyNo organising fake transactions, review manipulation or coordinated misleading promotion; verify host identity; day-to-day host management
Individual hostNo absolute claims, no fake limited-time offers, no exaggerated efficacy, no impersonating official endorsement, no defamatory statements about competitors

The practical consequence for a brand: if an agency supplies the host and the script, the brand is still the room operator, and the room-operator duties are the ones a brand most often fails — particularly "display prices and service items prominently and continuously" and "vet marketing language before broadcast."

The same document also requires platforms to place a merchant licence and qualification lookup entry in a prominent position on campaign pages — which means the credentials a brand submits during onboarding become publicly queryable during the festival.

5. Jinan: Pop-Up Ads, a Separate Front

The Jinan guide targets a narrow surface — the pop-up ads that appear on car-park QR payment pages — but its rules are the general pop-up standard applied concretely.

ProhibitionDetail
Ad identificationMust be clearly marked "广告" (advertisement). May not use "推荐", "温馨提示" or "特别关注" as substitutes
One-click closeA close control must be present and functional; no ads that only close after a countdown; no false, illegible or hard-to-locate close buttons; no closing that requires two or more clicks; no re-serving an ad after it is closed on the same page
Deceptive click triggersNo fake system or software update, error, cleanup or notification prompts; no fake play/pause/stop/return controls; no false reward promises
Four-party liabilityCar-park operator, advertiser, ad agency/publisher and third-party technical provider each carry defined duties

For advertisers, the transferable lesson is the substitution prohibition: "温馨提示" instead of "广告" is a labelling violation, not a stylistic choice. That standard applies well beyond car parks.

6. The Three Evasions Brands Are Actually Using

On 1 November, a consumer alert published by the China Consumers' Newspaper and the Anhui consumer council documented the specific techniques brands use to appear compliant while hiding AI generation. These are the patterns a compliance audit will look for.

EvasionHow it worksWhy it fails the standard
Illegible labellingA tiny corner note reading "AI effect for reference only" or "digital human display"The label must be explicit and prominent; small print in a corner is neither
Mixed presentationAI-generated model images arranged among a few real photographsThe consumer cannot tell which images are generated; the ad is misleading by composition
Motion misleadingAI-generated video optimises movement, garment drape and accessory fitActual wearing result differs materially; video needs a notice in the opening frame and surrounding area

The alert also names four broader AI-related consumer traps in this year's festival: AI-replicated celebrity faces in livestream loops, AI-fabricated buyer reviews and rankings, AI investment-return scams, and the mistaken belief that AI authenticity-checking tools carry legal weight.

The most useful line for a brand: an AI model image mixed into real photographs is not a defensible middle ground. It is the specific pattern regulators are now describing as a trap.

7. Why the Timing Matters More Than the Wording

Chinese advertising regulation has historically operated on a pre-campaign rhythm: publish guidance, let the market prepare, then inspect.

These three documents invert that. They were issued at 20:00 on opening night and the following day — after creative was live, after budgets were committed, after hosts were booked.

Three consequences follow:

First, "we prepared before the campaign" is no longer a defence. The standard being applied is the one published during the campaign.

Second, remediation speed becomes the operative capability. A brand that can swap a creative asset, re-label a digital-human stream or correct a price display within hours is exposed far less than one that needs a three-day approval cycle.

Third, the three-year retention requirement makes everything auditable retrospectively. A livestream replay from opening night is still discoverable in 2029.

8. Three Years of Retained Records

The retention clause deserves separate attention because it changes what "clean up after the campaign" means.

Guangzhou requires price records, livestream replays, orders and complaint materials retained for at least three years. In practice that means:

  • Price history must be reconstructable, which is what makes a "raise price then discount" finding provable months later
  • Livestream replays become evidence, so a host's verbal claim is as auditable as the product page
  • Complaint files create a documented trail of what the brand knew and when

A brand that keeps only what the platform dashboard retains is not compliant. Platforms retain for their own purposes and on their own schedules; the obligation here sits with the operator.

9. Reading This as a Fourth Governance Tier

For readers who have followed this series, these documents sit at a level we had not covered.

TierInstrumentLevelTiming
MinistryFive Excellence notice on e-commerce qualityNational, cross-platformBefore campaign
IndustryAIGC advertising governance blueprintIndustry self-regulationBefore campaign
PlatformWeChat traffic-diversion rulesSingle platformStanding
Local regulatorThese three documentsProvincial and municipalDuring campaign

The tier matters because local market supervision bureaus are the bodies that actually inspect and fine. A ministry notice sets direction; a municipal guidance opinion tells you what the inspector in that city is looking at this week.

A caveat worth stating plainly: local guidance is weaker authority than a ministry measure. But three cities publishing within seventy-two hours, all restating the same national labelling rule, is not coincidence — it is coordinated timing around a national priority.

11. Key Takeaways

  • Three regulators published Double 11 compliance documents between 30 October and 31 October — Sichuan-Chongqing jointly, Guangzhou, and Jinan — while the campaign was already running.
  • The AI labelling obligation is not new. The Measures for Labelling AI-Generated Synthetic Content took effect 1 September 2025, requiring explicit and implicit labels on AI-generated text, images, audio, video and virtual scenes.
  • Video has a placement rule, not just a presence rule: a prominent notice in the opening frame and the area surrounding playback.
  • Guangzhou names AI marketing and digital-human livestreams directly, and requires them to be prominently disclosed as AI-generated.
  • Sichuan-Chongqing splits livestream liability into four duty holders — platform, room operator, host agency and individual host — and the brand is the room operator even when an agency supplies the host.
  • Three specific evasions are now documented: illegible corner labels, AI model images mixed among real photographs, and AI video that optimises drape and movement.
  • Pricing transparency now requires separating product price, platform discount and government subsidy into distinct disclosures.
  • Records must be retained for at least three years — price history, livestream replays, orders and complaints — which makes campaign conduct auditable long after it ends.
  • The timing is the substantive change. Guidance published during a campaign replaces guidance published before it, so "we prepared in advance" stops being a defence and remediation speed becomes the real capability.
  • This is a fourth governance tier below ministry and industry instruments: local market supervision bureaus are the bodies that inspect and fine, and three cities acting within seventy-two hours indicates coordinated timing rather than coincidence.
  • This article summarises published regulatory documents and a consumer alert for information purposes and is not legal advice. Local guidance is weaker authority than a national measure; confirm current requirements with the relevant regulator and with counsel before changing live campaign assets.
