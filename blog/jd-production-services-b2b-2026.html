The B2B Underneath Double 11: JD's RMB 300 Billion Production-Service Business

Every piece of Double 11 coverage this year says the same thing: platforms stopped competing on price and are each building a moat. That is correct and incomplete. JD's production-service business — services sold to enterprises rather than consumers — exceeded RMB 300 billion in 2025 and serves over 10 million corporate customers, covering 91% of Fortune Global 500 companies operating in China. That is a B2B business larger than most of the consumer platforms it competes with, and it sits underneath the festival rather than inside it. This article sets out what production services actually are, where the growth is concentrated, why the moat argument is structural rather than promotional, and three substantive counter-arguments that argue against the thesis.

1. The Story Nobody Is Covering

Every piece of coverage on this year's Double 11 tells the same story: platforms have stopped competing on price, and each is now building its own moat. Tmall is using AI to surface demand consumers have not expressed. Douyin is restructuring merchant support. JD is leaning into services.

That framing is correct and it is also incomplete, because the most consequential number JD disclosed this quarter is not a Double 11 metric at all.

JD's production-service business — services sold to enterprises, not consumers — exceeded RMB 300 billion in 2025, serving more than 10 million corporate customers. That is a B2B business larger than most of the consumer platforms it competes with, and it sits underneath the shopping festival rather than inside it.

This article is about that number, why it exists, what it is actually made of, and what it changes for a B2B brand selling into China. It is also, deliberately, about the parts of the story that argue against it.

2. What "Production Services" Actually Means

The term is 生产性服务 — services consumed by enterprises in the course of producing, operating and selling, as opposed to services consumed by households. China's National Bureau of Statistics maintains ten official categories, and JD claims coverage across all ten.

JD's structure rests on four supply chains plus a talent chain, with AI applications layered on top.

LayerWhat it covers
商流 — commerce flowProcurement, distribution, marketplace operations
物流 — logisticsWarehousing, fulfilment, cross-border (overseas warehouses in 26 countries and regions)
金融 — financeSupply-chain finance, leasing
技术 — technologyCloud compute, vertical AI models, industrial software
人才服务链 — talentBlue-collar training and staffing for service delivery

From these JD says it derives five major categories and more than 200 individual services, spanning R&D, production, operations, sales and after-sales, delivered across dozens of industries and hundreds of sub-scenarios.

The three value claims JD makes for the bundle are compliance transparency, cost and efficiency, and professional reliability — the vocabulary of enterprise procurement rather than consumer marketing.

3. The Numbers

These are JD's own disclosures from its 2026 Double 11 launch briefing. They should be read as company-stated figures, not audited data.

The coverage percentages are the striking part. A platform reaching 98% of China's top 500 private enterprises is not a side project — it is embedded infrastructure for the domestic corporate sector.

4. Where the Growth Is

The aggregate is large but static; the growth rates show where the business is actually moving.

SegmentGrowth

The two figures that matter most for a B2B marketer are in the industrial arm rather than the headline.

JD Industrial's JoyIndustrial large model already drives AI-attributed transactions equal to 6.5% of core customers' GMV, and JD states that by the end of next year its industrial AI capability will cover more than 80% of enterprise customers.

That last projection is the one worth planning against. If it holds, JD Industrial stops being an optional channel and becomes a default surface that a B2B brand's Chinese customers will already be using.

5. The Market: RMB 40 Trillion

The reason JD is pushing here is structural, not opportunistic.

MeasureValue
China's service-sector value added, 2025RMB 80.89 trillion
Of which production servicesOver RMB 40 trillion
Share of the economyServices are now the largest sector
Policy directionProduction services pushed toward specialisation and higher value-chain position

A RMB 40 trillion addressable market, a national policy pushing it up the value chain, and a manufacturing base generating demand for specialised services — that is the macro case, and it does not depend on Double 11 at all.

It also explains the timing. With the Five Excellence notice restricting "lowest price on the entire web" tactics, and with consumer traffic growth below 2% on a 937-million user base, the consumer side of the equation has stopped offering easy expansion. The enterprise side still has policy tailwind.

6. Why This Is the Moat, Not the Discount

The strategic argument is straightforward once the numbers are laid out.

Consumer-side competition is now structurally capped: Tmall and JD overlap on more than 80% of users and merchants, the total user base is growing under 2%, and regulation has closed the discount lever. Platforms cannot win on the same axis any more, which is why every one of them is describing a different moat this year.

JD's answer is not a better discount. It is a service network that a discount-based competitor cannot replicate quickly — self-operated blue-collar staff with certification and standard operating procedures, warehousing and fulfilment reaching 26 countries, and industrial procurement relationships embedded in 91% of the Fortune Global 500 operating in China.

The consumer services JD showcased at the festival — over 100 segmented products across home, mobility and health — are the visible tip. The enterprise services are the load-bearing structure.

7. The Counter-Case: What Could Go Wrong

An article built only on company disclosures is a press release. There are three substantive arguments against the thesis, and they are worth stating plainly.

RiskSubstance
The consumer-services push has not yet proven it can take shareThree years of price competition did not displace the "cheap on Pinduoduo, quality on JD" divide. The 2025 Double 11 growth in ordering users (+40%) and orders (near +60%) was subsidy-funded, and its durability is unproven
Service operations are a different business modelHome cleaning, eldercare and car care are operationally heavy, low-margin and geographically dense. JD's tens of thousands of service staff compare against Meituan's millions of riders and millions of active merchants — density, efficiency and fulfilment elasticity all need time to verify
Regulatory and liability exposure is higher than in goodsEldercare and at-home nursing carry far higher regulatory thresholds and personal-safety liability than selling a product. A single serious incident damages the brand more than a product recall would

There is a fourth, narrower caution: the coverage percentages are not the same as revenue concentration. Serving 91% of the Fortune Global 500 in China could mean large contracts, or it could mean small accounts at many of them. JD has disclosed the former, not the latter.

8. What It Means for B2B Brands

For a B2B brand selling into China, this is not a story about JD's strategy. It is a story about where your buyers already are.

DevelopmentWhat it changes
10 million enterprise customers on one platform's service networkYour Chinese buyers are already transacting there; the question is whether you are present in the same workflow
JD Industrial AI to cover 80%+ of enterprise customers by end-2027Industrial AI becomes a default procurement surface; absence from it becomes a visibility problem rather than a channel gap
AI-attributed transactions at 6.5% of core customer GMVThe attribution model for industrial marketing is shifting toward platform-mediated discovery
91% of the Fortune Global 500 operating in ChinaIf your parent or peers are on the list, your procurement competitors are already inside this network
Five categories and 200+ servicesThe bundle means one relationship can carry procurement, logistics, finance and software — reducing the number of vendors a buyer needs
Compliance transparency as a stated valueEnterprise procurement in China increasingly screens on compliance; a platform that markets it is competing for exactly that budget

The practical implication is a channel-audit question rather than a media-buying one: if your Chinese enterprise customers procure through JD Industrial, what does your presence there look like — a listing, a contract, or nothing?

9. A Note on Sourcing

Three cautions apply to every figure in this article.

First, the numbers are company-stated. The RMB 300 billion scale, the customer count, the coverage percentages, the 6.5% AI-attributed GMV and the 80% coverage projection all come from JD's own briefing, reported by state and trade media. They have not been independently audited. The market-size figures (RMB 80.89 trillion service sector, RMB 40 trillion production services) come from the National Bureau of Statistics and are independent.

Second, the growth rates and the scale are not the same period. The RMB 300 billion is a 2025 figure; the +33%, +27.4%, 7x and 9x growth rates are 2026 figures. Presenting them as one snapshot would be misleading.

Third, the counter-case is drawn from Chinese trade press analysis, not from JD. Where this article argues against the thesis, the reasoning is attributed to that analysis rather than presented as established fact.

11. Key Takeaways

  • JD's production-service business exceeded RMB 300 billion in 2025, serving over 10 million corporate customers — a B2B business sitting underneath Double 11 rather than inside it.
  • Coverage: 91% of Fortune Global 500 companies operating in China, 98% of China's top 500 private enterprises, and 70% of specialised SMEs.
  • The structure rests on four supply chains (commerce, logistics, finance, technology) plus a talent chain, producing 5 categories and 200+ services across all 10 official NBS production-service categories, with overseas warehouses in 26 countries and regions.
  • Growth is concentrated in the enterprise arms: JD Logistics corporate warehousing/fulfilment +33%, JD Industrial H1 2026 revenue +27.4%, corporate takeaway and auction over 7x and 9x.
  • JD Industrial's JoyIndustrial model drives AI-attributed transactions at 6.5% of core customer GMV, with a stated target of 80%+ enterprise customer coverage by end-2027.
  • The market is RMB 80.89 trillion in service-sector value added, of which production services exceed RMB 40 trillion, with national policy pushing the segment toward specialisation and higher value-chain position.
  • The moat argument is structural, not promotional: with 80%+ user overlap between Tmall and JD, sub-2% user growth, and regulation closing the discount lever, a self-operated service network is harder to replicate than a price position.
  • Three substantive counter-arguments exist: the consumer-services push has not proven share capture, service operations are operationally heavy and low-margin against Meituan's network density, and eldercare/nursing carry materially higher regulatory and liability exposure than goods.
  • A fourth caution: coverage percentages are not revenue concentration — serving 91% of the Fortune Global 500 in China may mean large contracts or many small accounts, and JD has not disclosed which.
  • For a B2B brand, the operative question is a channel-audit question: if your Chinese enterprise buyers procure through JD Industrial, what does your presence look like?
  • This article summarises company disclosures, official statistics and trade-press analysis for information purposes and is not investment advice. Scale, coverage and growth figures are JD-stated and unaudited; verify current terms with the platform before committing channel budget.
